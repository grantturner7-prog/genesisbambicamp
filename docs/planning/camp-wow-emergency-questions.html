Camp WOW safety plan

The short list: 28 points for Dave, James and Grant · 28 to 31 January 2027

A camp needs a short plan that everyone remembers. These 28 points are that plan. Go through them together and put a name beside each one. The long list of 106 questions is kept at the bottom of this page as a reference for the lawyer, the travel agency and the nurse. You do not need to work through it at the meeting.

Who can come

Your rules, Grant. The first two are now on the website and in the camp notice.

  1. No fever on the bus. Every camper’s temperature is taken at The Lin Hotel. A camper with a fever does not board and goes home to rest. Still to set: the temperature that counts as a fever.
  2. No serious medical conditions on this camp. Campers with severe asthma or another serious medical condition cannot join. The nurse reads every health form before camp.
  3. Put the right to say no in writing. The travel contract and the camp notice must say when we can turn a camper away and what is refunded. The rule is written; the refund amounts are still blank.
  4. What every camper brings. The health form, the health insurance card, their own medicine in its original bag, and two phone numbers that are answered at night.

Papers to have before camp

Six things to collect. Each one is a document, so it is either in the folder or it is not.

  1. The travel agency is the seller. It signs the contract with every parent, takes the payment, and its name and registration number are on every advert.
  2. Insurance copies. The agency’s liability insurance and performance bond; Bambi’s public liability insurance covering the lake, kayaks, tram and campfire; accident cover for each camper.
  3. Bambi’s registration. The number, and confirmation that it covers guests sleeping in the camper vans.
  4. The nurse’s Taiwan licence, and the name of a doctor she can phone during camp.
  5. Background checks. A police record certificate from every adult, and proof that every foreign counselor may work at this camp.
  6. One letter from a lawyer saying this is a tour and an outdoor camp, not a cram school.

On site

The basics that prevent most problems.

  1. One person in charge, one deputy. Every adult knows who they are.
  2. An emergency card for every adult. 119, 110, the camp address in Chinese, the nearest hospital, the nurse, the camp lead, Bambi’s night duty phone and the travel agency’s 24-hour phone.
  3. A Mandarin speaker on site at all hours to make the 119 call and talk to the hospital.
  4. A car and a named driver on site all four days, for hospital trips that do not need an ambulance.
  5. Head counts at fixed times. A roster with photos, and team bibs on during activities.
  6. One assembly point and one alarm signal. Walk the campers there on the first day.
  7. The lake. A life jacket on everyone, Bambi’s coach on the water, a counselor counting from the shore. No kayaking in rain, thunder or strong wind. Nobody near the water after dark.
  8. The campfire. One adult in charge, water and an extinguisher beside it, campers seated back from the fire.
  9. The camper vans. A working smoke alarm and carbon monoxide alarm in each. A counselor checks every van at lights-out, and campers know which van to knock on at night.
  10. Allergies. The list goes to the kitchen a week before camp, the nurse checks those plates, and an allergic camper’s injector goes wherever the camper goes.
  11. First aid. The kit stays with the nurse. Ask Bambi where the nearest AED is, and rent one if there is none. Name the counselors trained in CPR.
  12. Weather. One person checks the forecast every morning and evening and moves the day indoors when needed.

If something happens

Six short rules to learn by heart.

  1. A camper is not at a count. It is nearly always the toilet, the van or another squad. Check those, and have one counselor walk the lake side and the pool side. If the camper has not turned up once the camp has been checked, the camp lead decides what happens next.
  2. A camper is ill. The nurse decides. Fever or vomiting means the sick van and a parent collects. If two or more are ill after the same meal, phone the county health bureau and keep the food.
  3. A camper goes to hospital. One adult goes with them, the camp lead phones the parents, and the travel agency is told at once. It has to report serious incidents to the Tourism Administration within 24 hours.
  4. A camper says someone is hurting them. Listen, do not promise to keep it secret, tell the camp lead, and phone 113 within 24 hours.
  5. One voice. One person speaks to parents and to the media. No names or photos of a hurt child in any group chat.
  6. Write it down the same day: the time, the place, and who did what.

The full list: 106 questions, with the Taiwanese rule behind each one

How far to trust the legal column

This is a question list, not legal advice. The rules were gathered by research into Taiwanese law. Where a source was seen during that research, the row says “source seen”. The rest come from knowledge of the law and have not been checked against the current text, so article numbers and deadlines should be confirmed by the lawyer or the travel agency before anyone relies on them. Where a row says “by contract”, no law clearly applies to a private camp or a glamping site, so the safe route is to write that standard into the agreement with the venue and the travel agency.
