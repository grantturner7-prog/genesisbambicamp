Your question: is 21:15 to 06:30 enough sleep?

Yes, with care. It gives about 9¼ hours in bed. Sleep-medicine guidance is 9 to 12 hours for ages 6 to 12 and 8 to 10 hours for teenagers, so this is comfortable for the 13 and 14 year olds and at the low end for the 10 to 12 year olds. For three nights that is fine if we protect it:

  • A quiet half hour before lights out: showers, the van check, no games. Every day has a shower slot.
  • The rest in the vans after lunch stays in every day.
  • The Friday evening snack is light and finishes by 20:50.
  • Bambi’s own film runs to about 21:20 and its snack to 22:00, about 100 m from our vans, so windows stay closed on Friday and Saturday.
  • If the campers look tired on Saturday, move wake-up to 07:00 and breakfast to 07:30. Nothing else needs to change.

Your earlier worry was right about my first draft: lights out at 22:00 with a 07:00 start was too little for the younger ones.

1. The schedule

Thirty campers in three teams of ten, each in its own bib colour from Decathlon. For games that work better small, each team splits into two squads of five, which gives six squads: three matches at once, or six stations, with nobody sitting out. Squad scores add up to the team’s Color War score. Each squad has its own counselor; two more counselors float, plus the camp lead and the nurse. Times are when the group is in place. The hall is about 200 m from our vans, so counselors start each move and its headcount ten minutes before.

Bambi’s guest times are marked guests. ours means we have the place to ourselves. Items that need Dave’s yes are listed in section 7.

Safety basics that stay

  • Headcount by name from a printed roster after every move, at meals and at lights out.
  • If a camper is missing: everyone stops, a count by name, then two counselors check the water first (the dock, the lake edge, the pool), then the van, then the last place seen. The lead tells Bambi’s front desk.
  • Water: nobody goes to the lake edge or the pool without a counselor. The pool is out of bounds for the camp. Bambi lists it as open all year, so guests may be using it.
  • Kayaking in January: no kayaking in rain, drizzle, thunder or strong wind (Bambi’s rule). Agree a cold limit with the coach and the nurse beforehand. A coach is afloat and a counselor stands on shore with the throw line. About 40 minutes on the water, then dry clothes in the heated vans. A spare dry set and spare shoes go on the packing list.
  • The nurse is at the lake during kayaking. The other group has a first-aid kit, a phone and a counselor trained in CPR.
  • After dark: every camper has a torch (packing list), counselors wear a headlamp and a bright vest, and groups walk with a counselor at the front and the back.
  • Campfire: counselors tend the fire, everyone sits on the log ring, water and a burn kit are beside it.
  • Adults: no counselor is alone with one camper out of sight. Van checks are done by two counselors, who keep a list of who holds each van key. Bambi’s staff enter a camper van only with a counselor.
  • Guests: friendly contact is fine. If a guest’s attention to a camper worries a counselor (photos, gifts, an offered lift), the counselor steps in politely and tells the lead.
  • Emergencies: call 119, then the lead, then the family. One night-duty counselor carries the phone for the gate and an ambulance. The one-page emergency card goes in the counselor pack after the separate emergency conversation with Dave.

Storage and transport

How much there is: about 12 storage bins of 65 litres, four big carrier bags of soft things (throws, ponchos, bibs, T-shirts), and a few loose items. Together that is roughly 2 to 2.5 cubic metres and about 250 kg.

At home or at camp the bins stack three high in four stacks, on about one square metre of floor. Allow a second square metre along a wall for the loose items: the bundle of 30 poles (2 m long), the cornhole boards (120 × 60 cm), six hula hoops, the two mini goals, the nested buckets and the water containers.

Getting it there: it is too much for a car boot. Ask the coach company for the size of the luggage hold, because it also has to take 40 people’s bags. If the hold is under about 7 cubic metres, send the kit separately in a small van or a seven-seat car with the seats folded, on the Wednesday or early on Thursday, and ask Dave for a dry, lockable place near our vans.
