The camp in one page

  • 30 campers, ages 10 to 14, in three teams of ten. Each team wears its own Decathlon bib color.
  • For small games each team splits into two squads of five: six squads. Every squad has its own counselor.
  • Two more counselors float. Plus the camp lead and the nurse (a registered nurse, on site 24/7).
  • Bambi's staff supply the kayak coach, the tram driver and the kitchen. Our counselors run all the other activities.
  • Bambi Glamping is ours alone for all four days: no other guests. Our base is the vans in zones A and B, by the big lawn.
  • Daily rhythm: wake 07:00, van check 07:30, breakfast 08:00, activities from 09:00, dinner 18:00, lights out 22:00. Thursday and Sunday differ.
  • Three nights in the vans: Thursday, Friday, Saturday. A quiet half hour, then van check at 21:45. Lights out 22:00.
  • Thursday: meet at The Lin Hotel, Taichung, at 08:00. The bus leaves 08:30 and we arrive about 10:00. Sunday: the bus leaves camp at 10:00.
  • Rain: every indoor activity moves into the main dining hall. The hall is about 200 m from our vans.

Teams, squads and Color War

  • Color War is the contest between the three teams. It opens Thursday at 19:00. The finale starts Sunday at 08:40.
  • Squads score for their team. Squad scores add up to the team's Color War score.
  • Van check, Friday and Saturday at 07:30: two counselors score each van from 0 to 5. Every camper carries the van's score to their own team.
  • Multi-sports (Friday, Saturday): 3 points for a win, 1 for a draw, plus a fair-play point. All of it is added to the team's score.
  • Orienteering (Friday): points for each control found, plus a bonus for teamwork. Saturday's treasure hunt: the finds count for Color War.
  • Camp Olympics and the Games Arcade: every station's score goes on the Color War board.
  • Write your squad's points on a score sheet as soon as each activity ends. Clipboards are on the shopping list.
  • Final scores are announced at the Sunday prize-giving, 09:20.

Signals and words

  • The whistle signal is taught on the Thursday orientation walk, 10:30. The plan does not name the set. The set below is proposed.
  • One long blast = stop and freeze. Stop, look at your counselor and stay where you are.
  • Three short blasts = assemble. Go to your squad counselor.
  • Raised hand = silence. Raise a hand and wait. Campers copy you and go quiet.
  • Spoken stop word: "Stop!" Any counselor or camper may call it when something is unsafe. Everyone freezes.
  • On the water, a whistle means paddle back to shore. The website tells parents the same.
  • Carry your whistle every day, and after dark. Blow it for these signals and for emergencies only.
  • Radios (8): the lead, the nurse, the dock, the two floaters and one a team.
  • The assembly point is set at the pre-camp walk with Bambi's contact. Ask the lead where it is.

Headcount and the buddy rule

  • Count campers by name from the printed roster. Do it after every move, at every meal and at lights out.
  • Start each move, and its headcount, ten minutes early. The hall is about 200 m from our vans.
  • Also count at the 07:30 van check (Friday, Saturday), where campers stand at their vans. On Sunday, count by name as they board the bus at 09:50.
  • Buddy rule: every camper has a buddy. Buddies stay together and tell a counselor before they leave the group.
  • A camper is missing: everyone stops. Count by name. Squad counselors keep their squads together.
  • Two counselors check the water first: the dock, the lake edge, the pool. Then the van. Then the last place the camper was seen.
  • The camp lead tells Bambi's front desk.
  • Not found in ten minutes: the lead phones 110, then the family.

Water, fire and the dark

  • Nobody goes to the lake edge or the pool without a counselor. The pool is out of bounds for the whole camp.
  • Kayaking: Bambi's coach and three counselors. One team of ten on the water at a time, inside the area the coach marks.
  • The coach is afloat. One counselor stands on shore with the throw line. A counselor checks every life jacket.
  • Non-swimmers paddle in doubles, beside the coach or a counselor.
  • No kayaking in rain, drizzle, thunder or strong wind. The nurse is at the lake during kayaking.
  • Campfire: counselors tend the fire. Everyone sits on the log ring. Water and a burn kit stay beside it.
  • Counselors keep the lighters. Hands stay back from the flames. Bambi checks the pit before lighting and that it is out and cold after.
  • After dark every camper has a torch (flashlight). Spare torches are on loan for campers who forget theirs.
  • After dark counselors wear a headlamp and a bright vest. Walk with a counselor at the front and a counselor at the back of the group.
  • Glow hunt and treasure hunt: walking only. The lake-side path is closed with glow tape and a counselor stands there.

Weather calls

  • The camp lead makes the weather call before breakfast and again at 12:45. Do not move your squad for rain on your own, except for thunder.
  • Rain means the main dining hall: every wet-weather activity moves there. Friday and Saturday the lead gives the call at morning circle, 08:50.
  • Thunder or lightning: take your squad indoors at once, then tell the lead. Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder.
  • No kayaking in rain, drizzle, thunder or strong wind (Bambi's rule). The lake station becomes the Games Arcade in the hall.
  • If Thursday's kayaking is rained off, Saturday's lake session (11:10 to 12:40) becomes the kayak session.
  • Shelter Build goes ahead in light rain. In heavy rain, squads build table-top model shelters in the hall.
  • Light rain, no thunder: the Amazing Race becomes the Umbrella Treasure Hunt: 30 umbrellas, walking only, 40 minutes at most.
  • Heavy rain: the Amazing Race checkpoints move into the hall.
  • Orienteering: Cipher Camp and the Indoor Escape Room Race. Multi-sports, Camp Olympics and the finale: the Games Arcade and Minute-to-Win-It.
  • Evenings: campfires, the film and both night hunts move into the hall. S'mores wait for a dry evening.
  • Late January lows are about 12 °C. Warm drinks follow every water or evening block. Kayakers change into dry clothes at the vans.

If someone is hurt, ill or upset

  • Hurt or ill: take the camper to the nurse, or send for the nurse if the camper cannot walk. Stay with the camper.
  • The nurse is a registered nurse, on site 24/7, and decides what happens next.
  • Tell the camp lead second. The lead tells the family third. Do not phone parents yourself.
  • A real emergency: call 119 first (fire, ambulance, water rescue). Then the lead. Then the family.
  • At night one night-duty counselor carries the phone for the gate and an ambulance. The emergency card gives the name and number.
  • Each squad has a small first-aid kit, and one more is at the lake. The nurse brings the main kit.
  • Upset or homesick: stay close, listen and tell the lead. Talk in the open: no counselor is alone with one camper out of sight.
  • A camper says someone is hurting them: listen, do not promise to keep it secret, and tell the lead at once.
  • Take no photos of a hurt or upset child. Post no names or photos of a hurt child in any group chat.
  • Only the camp lead speaks to parents and the media. Write down what happened the same day: time, place, who did what.
